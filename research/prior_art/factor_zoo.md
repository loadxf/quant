## Synthesis

The factor zoo literature defines the prior against which any claim of a "new edge in daily US equity data" must be judged, and that prior is brutal. By 2016 Harvey, Liu and Zhu had catalogued 316 factors from 313 papers; by early 2019 the Harvey-Liu census reported "over 400 factors published in top journals" (382 through 2018 per CXO Advisory's reading of the census chart — see C6 caveat), and the Chen-Zimmermann open-source corpus we hold locally documents 212 replicated predictors plus 114 placebos with full construction details. Daily US equity data — CRSP prices plus Compustat fundamentals — is the single most strip-mined dataset in empirical social science. Chen, Lopez-Lira and Zimmermann make the point mechanical: brute-force mining of 29,000 accounting ratios reproduces the published literature's out-of-sample performance, meaning the marginal published paper adds little that a for-loop would not have found.

The replication debate refines rather than overturns this. Hou-Xue-Zhang's claim that 65% (82% under t>=2.78) of 452 anomalies fail is real but conditional on their filters: NYSE breakpoints and value-weighting, which kill anything that lives in microcaps — 96% of the trading-frictions category dies. Chen-Zimmermann show the originals nearly all reproduce as originally constructed (98% of clearly-significant ones), and Jensen-Kelly-Pedersen show the failure rate collapses to a ~84% success rate under CAPM alphas, capped value-weights and Bayesian shrinkage. The reconciled picture: published effects are mostly real in their original form, but concentrated in small/illiquid stocks, heavily overlapping (JKP compress 153 factors into 13 themes), and much smaller than reported once implementable weighting is imposed.

Decay is the second pillar. McLean-Pontiff's canonical numbers — returns 26% lower out-of-sample, 58% lower post-publication, with the 32-point gap attributed to publication-informed trading — quantify how quickly the market metabolizes public knowledge, and Jacobs-Müller show this arbitrage response is essentially a US phenomenon, i.e., strongest exactly in the market we are testing. The data-snooping critiques (Lo-MacKinlay 1990; Sullivan-Timmermann-White's 7,846 technical rules and 9,452 calendar rules) established decades ago that in daily price data, seemingly wildly significant patterns routinely dissolve under family-wise adjustment.

For this project the operational consequences are: (1) any candidate signal must first be screened against the 212 CZ predictors — and note 67 of them are built from price/volume/options data, the neighborhood a daily-bar project inhabits; (2) an in-sample t-stat of ~2 is close to meaningless — HLZ's t>3.0 is the field's stated minimum, and even t>3 survivors should be haircut ~25-50% for expected decay; (3) apparent novelty is cheap because the CZ corpus's discovery samples mostly end by 2005 — a "new" effect in 2010-2025 data may simply be an old effect's decayed residue or a regime artifact; and (4) the honest null hypothesis is that anything we find at conventional significance in this dataset is a false discovery until multiple-testing-adjusted, decay-discounted evidence says otherwise. Novelty claims in the REPORT must be phrased relative to this base rate, not relative to an empty field.
